Electricity Bill Calculator

Estimate your electricity bill using units consumed, fixed charges and slab-based electricity rates.

Tariffs differ by state, DISCOM and consumer category. This calculator provides an estimate and is not an official electricity bill.

What Is an Electricity Bill?

An electricity bill is generally based on the amount of electricity consumed during a billing period along with applicable fixed charges, taxes, fees and other charges.

Electricity tariffs can vary depending on your state, electricity distribution company (DISCOM), consumer category and applicable tariff rules.

What Does Electricity Consumption Mean?

Electricity consumption is commonly measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh), often referred to as units on electricity bills.

For example, using 1 kilowatt of electrical power continuously for 1 hour consumes 1 kWh, or 1 unit of electricity.

How Does Slab-Based Electricity Billing Work?

Some electricity tariffs use different rates for different levels of consumption. A lower rate may apply to consumption within the first slab, while a different rate may apply to units above that slab.

This calculator uses two rates: one rate for units within the first slab and another rate for units above the first slab.

Electricity Bill Calculation Formula

For consumption within the first slab:

Energy Charge = Units × First Slab Rate

If consumption exceeds the first slab:

Energy Charge = First Slab Units × First Rate + Remaining Units × Second Rate

The basic estimate produced by this calculator is:

Estimated Bill = Energy Charge + Fixed Charge

Electricity Bill Calculation Example

Suppose a household consumes 180 units. Assume the fixed charge is ₹100, the first 100 units cost ₹3 per unit, and units above 100 cost ₹5 per unit.

First 100 units = 100 × ₹3 = ₹300

Remaining 80 units = 80 × ₹5 = ₹400

Energy Charge = ₹300 + ₹400 = ₹700

Estimated Bill = ₹700 + ₹100 = ₹800

Important: Your Actual Bill May Be Different

This calculator provides a simple estimate based on the rates and charges entered by the user. Actual electricity bills can include additional components such as taxes, subsidies, fuel adjustment charges, meter charges, government levies and other applicable fees.

Tariffs and billing rules can also change over time. Always check your latest official tariff information and electricity bill for the exact amount payable.

Ways to Reduce Electricity Consumption

  • Switch off appliances when they are not being used.
  • Use energy-efficient lighting and appliances where practical.
  • Avoid leaving devices on standby unnecessarily.
  • Use air conditioners and coolers efficiently.
  • Monitor monthly electricity consumption to identify unusual increases.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is one unit of electricity?

One unit of electricity generally refers to one kilowatt-hour (kWh) of electricity consumption.

Why can my actual electricity bill be higher than the calculator result?

The actual bill may include additional charges, taxes, subsidies, adjustments or fees that are not included in this simple calculator.

Does every state use the same electricity tariff?

No. Electricity tariffs can vary by state, DISCOM, consumer category and applicable tariff structure.

Can I use this calculator for a household electricity bill?

Yes. You can use it for a basic household estimate when you know the applicable rates and fixed charges.

Is this an official electricity bill calculator?

No. This is an independent estimation tool. It does not generate an official electricity bill and should not be treated as an exact billing statement.
