EMI Calculator

Calculate your estimated monthly loan EMI, total interest and total repayment using the loan amount, annual interest rate and loan tenure.

This is an estimate. Actual lender calculations may include processing fees, insurance, taxes, other charges and different interest-rate terms.

What Is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the regular amount a borrower pays towards a loan, usually every month, during the repayment period.

A typical EMI consists of both principal repayment and interest. The proportion of principal and interest changes during the loan tenure.

EMI Formula

For a standard reducing-balance loan, the EMI can be calculated using:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

Where:

  • P = Loan principal
  • r = Monthly interest rate
  • n = Number of monthly instalments

The annual interest rate is converted into a monthly rate before applying the formula.

EMI Calculation Example

Suppose you take a loan of ₹5,00,000 at an annual interest rate of 9% for 60 months.

Loan Amount = ₹5,00,000

Interest Rate = 9% per year

Tenure = 60 months

The estimated monthly EMI is approximately ₹10,379.65.

The estimated total interest over the full tenure is approximately ₹1,22,779.00.

The estimated total repayment is approximately ₹6,22,779.00.

What Affects Your EMI?

  • Loan amount: A larger loan generally results in a higher EMI.
  • Interest rate: A higher interest rate generally increases the EMI.
  • Loan tenure: A longer tenure can reduce the monthly EMI but may increase total interest paid.
  • Repayment structure: Different loan products may use different calculation methods.

Shorter vs Longer Loan Tenure

A shorter loan tenure generally means higher monthly payments but can reduce the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

A longer tenure generally reduces the monthly EMI, but the total interest paid may be higher because the loan remains outstanding for a longer period.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does EMI stand for?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the regular payment made towards a loan.

Does a longer tenure reduce EMI?

Generally, a longer repayment tenure reduces the monthly EMI, but it can result in more total interest being paid over the entire loan.

Does a higher interest rate increase EMI?

Yes. Assuming the loan amount and tenure remain the same, a higher interest rate generally results in a higher EMI and higher total interest.

Can I calculate EMI for a home loan or personal loan?

Yes. This calculator can be used for loans that follow a standard reducing-balance EMI calculation.

What happens if the interest rate is 0%?

If the interest rate is 0%, the loan amount is simply divided by the number of monthly instalments.
