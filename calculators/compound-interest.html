Compound Interest Calculator

Calculate compound interest and the future value of an investment or principal amount using the interest rate, time and compounding frequency.

What Is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on the original principal as well as the interest accumulated during previous periods.

Because previously earned interest can also generate interest, compound growth can become significant over longer periods.

Compound Interest Formula

The standard compound interest formula is:

A = P × (1 + r / n)nt

Where:

  • A = Future value
  • P = Principal amount
  • r = Annual interest rate as a decimal
  • n = Number of compounding periods per year
  • t = Time in years

Compound interest can then be calculated as:

Compound Interest = Future Value − Principal

What Does Compounding Frequency Mean?

Compounding frequency tells you how many times interest is added to the balance during one year.

  • Annually = 1 time per year
  • Semi-annually = 2 times per year
  • Quarterly = 4 times per year
  • Monthly = 12 times per year
  • Daily = 365 times per year

The actual compounding frequency depends on the financial product or agreement being considered.

Compound Interest Example

Suppose you invest ₹10,000 at an annual interest rate of 8% for 5 years, compounded monthly.

Principal = ₹10,000

Annual Interest Rate = 8%

Time = 5 years

Compounding = 12 times per year

Using the compound interest formula, the future value is approximately ₹14,898.33.

The approximate compound interest earned is ₹4,898.33.

Compound Interest vs Simple Interest

With simple interest, interest is calculated only on the original principal. With compound interest, accumulated interest can also become part of the amount on which future interest is calculated.

Therefore, over longer periods, compound interest can produce a larger future value than simple interest when the other assumptions are the same.

Important Things to Remember

  • The calculator provides a mathematical estimate based on the values entered.
  • Actual investment returns may differ because of taxes, fees, changing rates or other conditions.
  • Higher compounding frequency can affect the calculated future value.
  • Inflation can reduce the purchasing power of money over time.
  • Always check the actual terms of a financial product before making financial decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between interest and future value?

Interest is the amount earned over the principal, while future value represents the principal plus the accumulated interest.

Does compound interest increase faster over time?

Compound growth can accelerate over time because accumulated interest can itself earn additional interest.

What does monthly compounding mean?

Monthly compounding means interest is applied 12 times during a year. Enter 12 in the compounding periods per year field.

Can I use a decimal interest rate?

Yes. You can enter rates such as 7.5% or 8.25%.
