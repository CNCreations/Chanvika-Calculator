Break-even Calculator

Calculate how many units you need to sell to cover your fixed and variable costs and reach the break-even point.

What Is the Break-even Point?

The break-even point is the level of sales at which your total revenue is equal to your total costs. At this point, the business has neither a profit nor a loss.

Businesses can use break-even analysis to understand how many products or services they need to sell before they start generating profit.

Break-even Formula

The basic break-even quantity formula is:

Break-even Units = Fixed Costs ÷ (Selling Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit)

The difference between selling price and variable cost is known as the contribution per unit.

Break-even Sales = Break-even Units × Selling Price per Unit

Break-even Calculation Example

Suppose a small business has fixed costs of ₹10,000. Each product is sold for ₹100 and the variable cost of producing one unit is ₹60.

Contribution per unit = ₹100 − ₹60 = ₹40

Break-even Units = ₹10,000 ÷ ₹40 = 250 units

Break-even Sales = 250 × ₹100 = ₹25,000

This means the business needs to sell approximately 250 units to cover the stated costs.

Fixed Costs vs Variable Costs

Fixed Costs

Fixed costs generally remain unchanged over a particular period even when the number of units sold changes. Examples can include rent, certain salaries and some recurring business expenses.

Variable Costs

Variable costs generally change according to the number of products produced or sold. Examples may include raw materials, packaging and per-unit production costs.

Why Is Break-even Analysis Useful?

  • Helps estimate the minimum sales volume needed to cover costs.
  • Can help with pricing and sales planning.
  • Helps businesses understand their contribution per unit.
  • Can be useful when planning a new product or business activity.
  • Provides a simple way to understand the relationship between costs, price and sales volume.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens after reaching the break-even point?

Once revenue exceeds the break-even level, the business can begin generating operating profit, assuming the underlying cost and pricing assumptions remain valid.

What if the selling price equals the variable cost?

If the selling price is equal to the variable cost, there is no contribution available to cover fixed costs, so a normal break-even quantity cannot be calculated.

What if the variable cost is higher than the selling price?

The business loses money on each unit before considering fixed costs. The selling price needs to be greater than the variable cost for this break-even calculation to produce a meaningful result.

Is break-even sales the same as profit?

No. Break-even sales represent the revenue level where total revenue equals total costs. At break-even, profit is zero.

Can small businesses use break-even analysis?

Yes. Break-even analysis can be useful for shops, retailers, manufacturers, service businesses and other small businesses.
